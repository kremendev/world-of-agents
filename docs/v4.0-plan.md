# v4.0.0: the Forge Keep in 3D

Plan only, from Dima's brief of 16 Nov ("v4 changes direction") and his notes of 17 Nov on the `forge-keep` prototype.
Nothing here is built, and nothing is built before he approves it. It replaces this file's earlier plan, the hall redrawn
as 2x pixel art, which is dropped; git history keeps it. v3.11.0 (the rest of the page on the Team page's tokens) folds
into part 4d; its plan stays as history.

**In one line:** the hall becomes the Forge Keep, a walled town of nine areas on a floating island that you turn, pan
and zoom around, ported from the working prototype on the branch `forge-keep`; the five agents are anime characters (cel
shading, ink outlines) who walk to where their real state puts them; and the page becomes a HUD floating over that
world, in the Team page's tokens.

**Decided** is Dima's. **Proposed** is Claude's, for his review. **Question** needs his answer before the part it names.
**Checked** is what Claude read on `forge-keep` at b11020a (17 Nov), by `git archive` into a scratch folder, never a
checkout.

## The prototype (Checked)

- **Code:** `town/src/`, plain JavaScript. `world.js` is pure data: rooms, spots, roads, the walking graph, `route()` and
  `routeFromNode()`. The 3D is ten modules, about 300 KB, that `build.py` joins into one HTML page by patching
  `base.html` as text (with asserts); `base.html` still holds an older map and 3D module the build cuts out. three r160
  comes from cdn.jsdelivr.net through an import map. Its checks are its own: `check.sh` (two page walks on a stand-in
  three, Playwright and Chromium), `compare.cjs`, `seats.cjs` and `shot.cjs` (real three r160, software WebGL). None of
  it is linted or in `npm run check`.
- **The world:** the nine areas (the town calls the War room the War hall), each with its doors, floor and spots; eight
  buildings nobody works in (barracks, store house, stable, granary, windmill, watch tower, shrine, camp), the dock, the
  altar, a stream and a pond, and, by its commit bcbe7a9, four far isles with trees from the Blender flora set. Houses
  open: a toggle folds their upper walls away, and the cut edges don't flicker (`separateCut()`).
- **The look:** `MeshToonMaterial` with a three-step gradient and a rim term patched into its shader
  (`onBeforeCompile`), inverted-hull ink on built shapes, and every frame two renders of the scene: one of normals and
  depth, then the scene itself into a 4x multisampled target; then a screen-space ink pass (silhouettes from depth,
  creases from normals), bloom and a colour grade that reads depth. PCF shadow maps. Early-evening light.
- **The characters:** five Tripo models, one mesh, one material and one JPEG beside it each, on a 65-bone `mixamorig`
  skeleton, with four clips: `idle`, `walk`, `sit` and `play_mobile_game` (the desk work). The town's poses map onto them
  (`CLIP_FOR`): typing and the orcs' smash play the phone clip; sitting and drinking play `sit`; sparring, painting,
  reading, pointing and planning play `idle`. Heights: the goblin 1.32 m, the elf 1.78, the female orc (Frontend dev)
  1.92, the reaper (Reviewer) 1.95, the male orc (Backend dev) 2.18. A seated model goes with its hips over the seat and
  two-bone IK bends its legs to the floor: 40 of 40 seats within 0.4 cm (`seats.cjs`). Each file keeps Tripo's AIGC
  label in `asset.extras`: the producer's company code and the model's ProduceID, nothing about the account.
- **The run:** its own 13-step script with four states (work, idle, needs, walking), not the app's nine states or
  `DEMO_RUN`.
- **Stand-ins:** every Blender model and every character has a hand-built stand-in (`m2b-gate.js`, the hand-built
  houses, `m6-cast.js`), because an artifact's fetch can fail or come late (20 s).
- **The files:** `.gltf.json`, the buffers inside as base64 `data:` URIs, because artifacts don't serve `.glb`; the page
  makes each into a GLB in memory before `GLTFLoader.parse()`. Packed by `tools/pack_gltf.py`: `KHR_mesh_quantization`
  required in all 22 files, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` used in 9 scenery files, no Draco, Meshopt or KTX2.
  `plainModel()` unpacks each as it loads.

## States and places

**Decided:**

| State                               | Place                 | What shows                        |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| writing, reading, thinking, waiting | the agent's work spot | the work, its screen lit          |
| needs-you                           | its work spot         | hand raised, and the HUD's banner |
| error                               | its work spot         | a marker over it                  |
| done, idle                          | idle life             |                                   |

Idle life: the orcs spar, drink or hammer; the Designer paints; the Lead wanders; the Reviewer reads. **The rule:** the
world never shows work that isn't happening, and a real event pulls the agent back at once. (`walking` is a state no
event sends, today too: it is what the world does between places.)

The places come from the app's own events through `officeReducer()` and the place map, never from the town's script.

**Decided (Dima, 17 Nov):**

- **Idle never stands in a work place.** Idle life only in the idle areas (Tavern, Training yard, Courtyard, Garden),
  never in a work room or at the Forge, which counts as a work place for idle (agents still walk through it, as the hub
  of the paths). An orc hammering by the furnace, the Designer painting in the studio or the
  Reviewer reading in the Library would look like work. So the orcs hammer at the Training yard's anvil, the Designer
  paints at an easel in the Garden, the Reviewer reads on a Courtyard bench or by the Tavern fire, and the Lead wanders
  the outdoor paths, never into a work room.
- **So the prototype's idle spots in work places go (Checked).** Its `IDLE` table sends idle agents into work places:
  the Reviewer only to the Library (`lib-chair`, `lib-shelf`), the Lead to the War hall's rug (`war-rug`), the Designer
  to the studio's easel (`studio-easel`), the Frontend dev to the Forge's anvil (`forge-anvil`); the built page even
  starts four of the five there. The port drops those spots from idle life. The town already has places for most of it
  (the Garden's easel and pond, the Training yard, the Tavern, the Courtyard's bench and well); the Reviewer gets a
  reading spot outside the Library, and the Training yard an anvil (Checked: the town's are at the Forge and in a
  house).
- **The desk hold:** an agent whose work ends less than 3 s after it sat down (2d's `MIN_DESK_MS`) stays seated until
  those 3 s are up, but its screen goes dark and its work animation stops at once.
- **The Reviewer walks to the dev's desk to review:** a Reviewer reading another agent's task reviews at that dev's
  desk, because that is work. (The town's script has it at the Library desk only.)
- **A waiting Lead stands in the War room**, in Live too (today's 2D hall puts it on the dev room carpet).
- **A Lead reading a dev's task goes to the Workshop**, as today (`CHECK_SPOT`, the v1 spec's "the Lead walks to the dev
  room to check progress", the demo's "Lead checks in"): that is work, and it happens there.

**Proposed**, so the rule holds:

- **"At once" means from where it stands.** A work event ends the idle activity on the spot, and the agent walks
  straight back, turning mid-path if it was walking. Today's 2D walker finishes a walk before it takes the next one; the
  town's graph already routes from any node (`routeFromNode()`), so turning mid-path is a port, not new work. That walk
  goes at the 2.5x hurry, as today.
- **Carried over unless Dima says otherwise:** the devs fetch their task scroll from the War room before going to their
  desks; the orcs smash their keyboards while writing ("!!"; no clip yet, so the town plays the phone clip with sparks
  over the keyboard); a message's bubble never shows while its agent walks, and its time runs only while it is on
  screen; small talk between two or more idle agents in the same idle area (our flavour text, never agent output).

**The Forge (Decided):** the furnace still shows how busy the keep is. Screens glow only while their owner works.
**Proposed:** today's furnace rules in 3D. The mouth's colour follows 0 / 1 / 2+ working agents, and the glow grows up to
4, as a glowing material and one light. Fires and candles flicker, each in its own phase. With reduced motion the
flicker stops, but the mouth's colour still changes, because it is the busy indicator. (The town's furnace already burns
hotter the more agents work; its lights and flames are hand-built, and stay so.)

## Camera

**Decided:** rotate, pan, zoom, a button per room, and a click on an agent opens its card, with Follow. Reduced motion:
no camera flights, agents move instantly, no idle animation.

The town has these (Checked): `OrbitControls`, an overview and a view per room with a flight between them, Follow, and
picking. The port takes them.

**Proposed:**

- Limits on distance and angle, so the camera stays above the ground and can't lose the keep.
- A room button flies the camera to that room's view in about a second (with reduced motion it cuts). Follow keeps the
  agent in view as it walks, until the camera is moved by hand or Follow is pressed again.
- A click on an agent is a raycast on its figure. The HUD's agent buttons do the same thing for the keyboard and screen
  readers, so nothing needs the mouse; the arrow keys pan when the canvas has focus.
- **The cutaway comes too**, which this plan didn't have: without it, the work rooms' insides sit under roofs. Its
  toggle becomes a HUD button.

## Tech

**Decided:**

- three.js as an npm dependency, pinned to an exact version and bundled by Vite. No CDN at runtime: the page still loads
  only from its own hall (`connect-src 'self'`), and the hall's one network call stays the GitHub one.
- The scene is its own module; the HUD stays React.
- Without WebGL, the page falls back to today's 2D hall.
- Rendering pauses while the tab is hidden; the pixel ratio is capped at 2.
- **three's version (17 Nov):** the newest release, `@types/three` to match, proven by a real-render comparison against
  the town's r160 renders, not only the logic checks. (The town runs r160, December 2023, and its checks ran against it.)
- **The 2D hall is frozen (17 Nov)** as the fallback: it keeps working and keeps its tests, but new world features
  aren't drawn there. That works because of the accessibility rule: everything the world shows is also in the HUD.

**Proposed:**

- **The render comparison:** the same views (the overview, each area, a house opened, a seated character) rendered by
  the town on `forge-keep` at r160 (its `shot.cjs`) and by the port at the newest release, in the same headless Edge,
  window size and pixel ratio, with the clock stopped and the agents on the same spots. Each pair compared pixel by pixel
  against a tolerance, and every pair looked at: a difference is either a three change, written down, or fixed. Where
  the port changes the world on purpose (no stand-ins, the idle spots), the view leaves it out.

**Checked (4a step 3, 18 Nov): the render comparison passes.** Eleven views (the overview, a side view, each house,
the Forge, the yard, the courtyard, the garden), the town at r160 and the port at 0.187.0, each in headless Edge with
software WebGL, 1100 x 700 at a pixel ratio of 1, reduced motion, the town's figures hidden, the views in one order (a
house's cutaway depends on the last view), eight frames each. The rule, set before any result: at most 1% of a view's
pixels differ by more than 16/255 in a channel, and the mean difference is at most 2/255. All eleven pass: at most
0.044% of pixels (the War room) and a mean of at most 0.19/255. The town rendered twice differs from itself in 16
pixels over all eleven views (its particles use `Math.random`). On the way there:

- One change after r160 was fixed in the port. `ShaderPass` clones its uniforms, and a depth texture's clone now gets
  a source of its own (three#30540), so the line and grade passes read an empty depth: the port hands them the real
  one. In the town at r160 the clone shared the source, so its silhouettes and its depth of field work; the
  normals' render-target texture was dropped by the clone in both, so the town never drew its crease lines, and the
  port keeps them off to match (a question below).
- The bloom: 0.187's `UnrealBloomPass` differs from r160's in four ways (the blur, the bright pass's weights, the
  composite and the blend; `src/world/scene/bloom.ts` names each), and its glow was visibly smaller and dimmer around
  every light. The port puts r160's back (a question below).
- Left, each a three change or a known one, every cluster raycast in the town to the object it falls on:
  - **Shadows:** 0.187's `PCFShadowMap` is new: a depth texture compared in hardware on a Vogel disk turned per pixel
    by noise, where r160 compared packed depth on a fixed grid. Surfaces lit at a grazing angle (the blossom trees'
    leaves, the studio's paper, the tavern's boards) self-shadow in a slightly different pattern. Proven: with the sun
    casting no shadow in both, every one of those clusters is gone.
  - **Lettering:** the task board and the signs. The town loads M PLUS Rounded 1c from Google Fonts, which the page's
    policy refuses, so the port drew them in the self-hosted Inter, the stack's next font. Since Dima's answer (18 Nov)
    the port carries the font itself (below). **Checked (18 Nov),** with the tools in `tools/world-check/` (which
    render the town exactly as step 3 did, 0 pixels off): the task board now matches the town (the War room's view
    0.025% of pixels, max 42/255, where Inter left 0.044% and 197). The TAVERN sign differs a little more than before
    (the tavern's view 0.021%, was 0.019%): the town paints its signs once as it builds, before its Google font has
    come, so its signs are in the fallback sans-serif, and only its task board redraws when the font arrives. The
    port loads the font first, so both are in M PLUS, as decided.
  - Specks of the particles, and two pixels in the garden where a glowing part's edge meets the stone 1 cm behind it.

- **Where the code goes:** `src/world/` holds pure TypeScript: the town's layout, spots, roads and path graph from
  `world.js`, the state-to-place map, idle life's schedule and the camera's views, all tested in Vitest with no WebGL.
  `src/world/scene/` holds three.js alone, ported from the town's modules. React talks to the scene through one small
  interface (mount, update with the office state, select, follow, fly to a room, open houses, dispose). The scene never
  imports React, and React never touches a three object.
- **Loaded on demand:** the scene and three come in through a dynamic `import()`, so the HUD and the 2D fallback don't
  wait for them. three will be the biggest part of the page; 4a measures it and writes it down.
- **WebGL 2:** three.js renders with WebGL 2 only. The page asks for a WebGL 2 context first. With none, with a scene that
  fails to load, or with a lost context that doesn't come back, it shows the 2D hall. jsdom has no canvas context
  (`setup.ts` returns null), so every test that exists today keeps running on the 2D hall.
- **One path, no stand-ins.** The town's hand-built stand-ins exist because an artifact's fetch can fail. In the app the
  models ship with the page from its own origin, so for every part that has a model the port takes the model alone, and
  a model that fails to load shows the 2D hall, as no WebGL 2 does. Hand-built stays where there is no model: the island's
  top, the sky, the roads, the props, the scatter, and the lights and flames (they animate). That leaves out
  `m2b-gate.js`, the hand-built houses and walls, and `m6-cast.js`'s figures (whatever of its rigs moves the models,
  4b keeps).
- **Cel shading and outlines:** the town's (Checked, above): `MeshToonMaterial` with its gradient and rim term, the
  inverted hulls, the screen-space ink pass, and the characters' JPEGs on toon materials. This replaces the plan's
  `OutlineEffect`. `onBeforeCompile` and `ShaderPass` take GLSL text, which WebGL compiles; no JavaScript is evaluated.
- **Performance:** the town draws far more than this plan proposed (two renders of the scene a frame, a 4x multisampled
  target, shadow maps, bloom, the ink pass and the grade, where the plan had blob shadows and no post-processing).
  Proposed: port its look whole, then measure a frame on this PC at 100%, 125% and 150% Windows scaling in 4a and write
  the numbers down; if it is too slow, the shadow maps go first, then bloom's resolution, before anything that makes
  the style. One loop through `renderer.setAnimationLoop`, stopped on `visibilitychange`;
  `Math.min(devicePixelRatio, 2)`, as the town has.
  **Checked (4a step 5, 18 Nov):** on this PC (RTX 5070, a 2560 x 1440 display at 59 Hz), the Pages page in headless
  Edge on the GPU, the frame rate unlocked, 10 s after a warm-up: 100% (stage 2153 x 1124 device px) 8.9 ms a frame,
  95% under 10.3 ms; 125% and 150% the same, 9.0 ms and 10.2 ms, since the device pixels hardly change. That is about
  half of a 59 Hz frame, so nothing goes. Not measured: the PC's integrated AMD Radeon, or any other machine. Size:
  three and the scene are their own chunk, 860 KB (235 KB gzipped), loaded by the dynamic import; the page's own
  script is 496 KB (143 KB gzipped), 3 KB more than before; the models 7.04 MB.
- **Labels:** name tags and bubbles stay HTML over the canvas, placed from 3D each frame (the town does this with
  `textContent`), `aria-hidden`, with today's overlap rules (`settleLabels()`) and rem minimums. They are moved through
  `element.style`, which the CSP allows, never through a `style` attribute.

- **GLB, not `.gltf.json`.** The town's format is an artifact workaround. A GLB is a quarter smaller, and a `.gltf` whose
  buffers are `data:` URIs makes `GLTFLoader` fetch them, which `connect-src 'self'` refuses (to confirm in 4a's Edge
  check). The conversion keeps `asset.extras` (the AIGC label) and the JPEG as its own file. `plainModel()` is ported as
  is: the packing stays.
- **The clips (Checked):** four per character. The plan's list also wants read, raise hand, the keyboard smash, and idle
  life's spar, drink, hammer and paint; with the four, those show as `idle`, `sit` or the phone clip.
- **References are references.** The models are our own characters in the cast's roles. A reference image of someone
  else's character guides the style, never a copy: original fantasy only, no game's or show's characters, names, emblems
  or art. The style rules stay as they are.
- **No Git LFS.** A plugin installed from GitHub is a git clone, and whether a clone fetches LFS files depends on the PC's
  git setup. Plain files avoid that question, so models are committed when final and changed rarely: every new export is
  a new blob in history forever.
- **The checks' tools (Decided, 18 Nov):** `tools/world-check/` (its README has the commands): `render.mjs` and
  `compare.mjs` for the render comparison, `policy.mjs`, `serve.ts` and `models-page.mjs` for the policy checks,
  `frames.mjs` for a frame's time, `town.mjs` for the town's test copy. Run by hand, never in `npm run check`.
- `.gitattributes` gets `*.glb binary` and `*.jpg binary`, so no line-ending conversion ever touches one. `PAGE_TYPES`
  (today neither) gets `.glb` as `model/gltf-binary` and `.jpg` as `image/jpeg`, and `readManifest()` keeps refusing
  everything else. 4f checks that `tests/invisible.test.ts` leaves binary files out.
- **Where they live (Proposed):** `src/world/models/`, each GLB and JPEG imported with Vite's `?url`, so the bundle names
  them and the bundled hall's manifest lists them. A GLB names its JPEG by file name, which the build's hashed names
  break, so the loader's URL modifier maps it through a fixed table built from those imports, never from a name the file
  gives. (Or `public/models/`, unhashed; 4a picks, with the reason.) The copy in `plugin/hall/page/` is the same bytes,
  so git stores it once; 4f checks that.
- **The tools come too (Proposed):** `town/blender/` (the scenery's source) and `town/tools/` (slimming and packing) into
  `tools/models/`, outside lint as `town/` is, with a GLB writer in place of `to_gltf_json.py`. Python on Dima's PC,
  never in `npm run check`. `compare.cjs` runs when a model is packed again.

## Security

**Decided:** models are our own bundled files only, loaded with three's `GLTFLoader`. No remote models, no eval, and the
CSP stays as it is.

**Proposed**, to keep "the CSP stays as it is" true:

- **No images packed into a GLB.** `GLTFLoader` turns an image packed inside a GLB into a `blob:` URL and loads it from
  there, and the policy (`default-src`, `img-src` and `connect-src`, all `'self'`) doesn't allow `blob:`. The models
  already keep this (Checked): the scenery has no images, and each character's only image is its JPEG, a file beside it,
  served from the page's own origin. 4a checks it in Edge with two test models under the policy: one with a packed image
  must fail, and one without must load. If that turns out wrong, `blob:` still doesn't go into the policy without Dima's
  say.
- **No compressed models:** no Draco, Meshopt or KTX2. Their decoders are WebAssembly, and Draco's runs in a worker made
  from a blob, so they would need `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` or `worker-src blob:`. The models keep this too (Checked): plain
  glTF, `KHR_mesh_quantization` and `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` only, both read by `GLTFLoader` with no decoder.
- three.js uses no `eval` and no `new Function`. 4a checks it the way Zod's jitless mode was checked: the hosted demo's
  meta policy reports nothing.
- **Nothing an agent sends becomes a model, a texture, a URL or a 3D text mesh.** Agent text stays React text in the HUD
  and in HTML bubbles. A model's URL comes from our bundle, never from an event, and so does the JPEG a GLB names.
- **The dependency:** an exact pin (no `^`), `npm audit` clean, and any install script blocked like every package's (three
  should have none). `@types/three` is pinned to match. An upgrade is a task of its own.
- The Pages demo carries the same policy as a meta tag, and the scene must load there too.
- **The AIGC label stays** (Checked: the producer's company code and the ProduceID, nothing about the account).
- **Flag, outside the app: the Blender runner.** While its window is open it runs any `.py` that lands in
  `Documents\WoA-models\jobs`, as Dima, with Blender's full Python. Anything that can write a file there can run code:
  a download, a script, an agent's edit he approves. The guard doesn't cover that folder. The README already says to
  close it after a batch. Proposed, for `tools/models/`: the runner runs only jobs whose SHA-256 is in a list it reads
  at its start.

**Checked (4a step 5, 18 Nov)**, in headless Edge, every policy violation recorded by an init script (which the policy
doesn't govern), each check with a control:

- **A packed image is refused.** Two models through three 0.187's `GLTFLoader` on a page served with the hall's own
  header (`HALL_CSP`): `bridge.glb` loads whole; a one-triangle GLB with a 1 x 1 PNG packed inside loads its triangle,
  but its image is refused (`connect-src blob`: 0.187 fetches a packed image from a `blob:` URL, so `connect-src`, not
  `img-src`, refuses it) and `GLTFLoader` says "Couldn't load texture". The failure is the texture's, not the model's: the
  model would draw without it, so `tests/models.test.ts` (no packed image) stays the guard. The same page with no policy
  loads the texture, so the refusal is the policy's.
- **No eval:** the Pages build (`VITE_LIVE=false`, base `/world-of-agents/`, its meta policy) shows the world with no
  violation at all and nothing in the console, where any `eval` or `new Function` would have been refused and reported.
- **The plugin's own page** (`plugin/hall/page`, as an install carries it) under the hall's header: the world shows, no
  violation.
