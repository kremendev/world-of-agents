# v4.0.0: the hall and the agents, redrawn in detail

Plan only, from Dima's brief of 16 Nov ("PLAN, DON'T BUILD", part D). Its own milestone, after v3.11.0. Nothing here is
built or drawn. It starts with a style mockup from Claude (design), and nothing is decided about who draws until Dima has
seen it. Original fantasy only: orcs, elves, goblins and skeletons are generic; no World of Warcraft or Blizzard names,
emblems, factions or art.

**In one line:** the Forge Hall and the five agents get much more detailed pixel art, drawn as PNG sprite sheets instead
of text rows, with the hall's rooms, cast, behaviour and labels kept exactly as they are.

## Where the art is today

- 43 sprites as text rows (`{ rows, palette }`, `.` transparent): 5 characters (about 20 art px wide), 10 workstation
  parts, 28 hall pieces, in `src/assets/` (1,948 lines). `validateSprite()` checks each.
- The hall is 672 x 480 art px, shown at the largest scale that fits the first screen, a sharp (whole-number) scale when
  one is within 15%. Labels are HTML over the canvas. Draw order by `z`; one `requestAnimationFrame` loop; the floor
  baked once.
- The page's CSP already allows images from the page itself (`img-src 'self'`).

## Step 1: the style mockup (Claude, design)

What it must show, side by side with today's art at the same screen size: the dev room with the Designer, the Frontend
dev and the Backend dev at their desks, one orc mid keyboard-smash; the Lead/PM in its room by the dashboard; the
Reviewer with its candle in the library nook; the furnace lit; and the cast on their own, front and side, idle and
walking. It also proposes the new art resolution (below), because that sets everything else. Dima approves it, or asks
for another, before step 2.

(file, author, licence, link), checked before each release.

## Step 3: from text rows to PNG sprite sheets

- **Format.** One PNG sheet per character and per group of props, with a JSON atlas: each frame's rectangle, its anchor
  (the feet point for characters, the base for props, which today's `z` uses), and named animations with frame times.
  Aseprite's own JSON export (`frames`, `meta.frameTags`) as the format if Aseprite draws it; otherwise the same shape,
  written by hand.
- **Loading.** Imported by Vite as URLs (same origin, so the CSP stays as it is), decoded once with `createImageBitmap`,
  baked into off-screen canvases as the text sprites are now. Drawn with `imageSmoothingEnabled = false`.
- **Resolution.** More detail means more art pixels: likely the hall at 2x (1344 x 960 art px), which halves every sharp
  scale that fits. The mockup's resolution decides; `fitPixelScale()` and the 15% sharp-scale rule are re-checked at
  100%, 125% and 150% Windows scaling, and the layout tests' placements scale with it.
- **The move, room by room.** `draw.ts` learns a second sprite source (sheet frames) beside text rows; each room or cast
  member switches when its art is ready, so the hall always works. The text sprites and `validateSprite()` go only when
  nothing uses them.
- **Checks.** A test for every sheet: its atlas frames inside the image, anchors inside their frames, no frame
  overlapping another, only fully clear or fully solid pixels (crisp edges), and the sizes the atlas says. The existing
  layout tests (props in the hall, agents in their rooms, nothing over a desk) keep running on the new placements.
- **Size.** A budget for the hosted demo: all art under 1.5 MB, each PNG optimised losslessly.
- **What doesn't change:** rooms, cast and names, states and their bubbles, walking and routes, the hurry and desk time,
  lights and flicker, the furnace's glow rule, reduced motion, and the HTML labels and buttons over the canvas.

## Security

Static images from the page's own origin; nothing from agents or the network becomes an image; the CSP doesn't change.
A licence file is the new duty: a release checks `docs/ASSETS.md` names every PNG.
