# v4.0.0: the Forge Keep in 3D

Plan only, from Dima's brief of 16 Nov ("v4 changes direction"). Nothing here is built, and nothing is built before he
approves it. It replaces this file's earlier plan, the hall redrawn as 2x pixel art, which is dropped; git history keeps
it. v3.11.0 (the rest of the page on the Team page's tokens) folds into part 4d; its plan stays as history.

**In one line:** the hall becomes a 3D keep of nine areas that you turn, pan and zoom around, the five agents become
anime characters (cel shading, ink outlines, chibi proportions) who walk to where their real state puts them, and the
page becomes a HUD floating over that world, in the Team page's tokens.

**Decided** is Dima's (16 Nov). **Proposed** is Claude's, for his review. **Question** needs his answer before the part
it names.

## Decided (Dima, 16 Nov)

- Real 3D with a rotating camera, in a detailed anime style: cel shading, ink outlines, chibi proportions. The 2x pixel
  redraw is dropped.
- Dima sends character references later, and the models are made from them. Until then, primitive figures.
- The rest of this file marks what else he decided.

## States and places

**Decided:**

| State                               | Place                 | What shows                        |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| writing, reading, thinking, waiting | the agent's work spot | the work, its screen lit          |
| needs-you                           | its work spot         | hand raised, and the HUD's banner |
| error                               | its work spot         | a marker over it                  |
| done, idle                          | idle life             |                                   |

Idle life: the orcs spar, drink or hammer; the Designer paints; the Lead wanders; the Reviewer reads. **The rule:** the
world never shows work that isn't happening, and a real event pulls the agent back at once. (`walking` is a state no
event sends, today too: it is what the world does between places.)

**Proposed**, so the rule holds:

- **Idle life only in the idle areas** (Tavern, Training yard, Courtyard, Garden), never in a work room or at the Forge.
  An orc hammering by the furnace, the Designer painting in the studio or the Reviewer reading in the Library would look
  like work. So the orcs hammer at the Training yard's anvil, the Designer paints at an easel in the Garden, the Reviewer
  reads on a Courtyard bench or by the Tavern fire, and the Lead wanders the outdoor paths, never into a work room.
- **"At once" means from where it stands.** A work event ends the idle activity on the spot, and the agent walks
  straight back, turning mid-path if it was walking. Today's 2D walker finishes a walk before it takes the next one; in
  3D the paths are a graph, so turning mid-path is simple, and the rule needs it. That walk goes at the 2.5x hurry, as
  today.
- **The 3 s desk time (2d).** Today an agent whose work ends less than 3 s after it sat down keeps showing that state for
  up to 3 s, so it doesn't bounce. Under the new rule it may stay seated those 3 s, but its screen goes dark and its work
  animation stops at once. **Question** (before 4b): keep the seat hold that way, or drop it.
- **Places that depend on the task.** Today a Reviewer reading another agent's task reviews at that dev's desk, a Lead
  reading one stands in the dev room, and a waiting Lead in Live stands there too (`destination.ts`). Proposed: keep
  them, counting that desk and the Workshop as the work spot for that task, because the work really happens there.
  **Question** (before 4b): that, or the strict map (the Reviewer always in the Library, the Lead always in the War room).
- **Carried over unless Dima says otherwise:** the devs fetch their task scroll from the War room before going to their
  desks; the orcs smash their keyboards while writing ("!!"); a message's bubble never shows while its agent walks, and
  its time runs only while it is on screen; small talk between two or more idle agents in the same idle area (our flavour
  text, never agent output).

**The Forge (Decided):** the furnace still shows how busy the keep is. Screens glow only while their owner works.
**Proposed:** today's furnace rules in 3D. The mouth's colour follows 0 / 1 / 2+ working agents, and the glow grows up to
4, as a glowing material and one light. Fires and candles flicker, each in its own phase. With reduced motion the
flicker stops, but the mouth's colour still changes, because it is the busy indicator.

## Camera

**Decided:** rotate, pan, zoom, a button per room, and a click on an agent opens its card, with Follow. Reduced motion:
no camera flights, agents move instantly, no idle animation.

**Proposed:**

- three's `OrbitControls`, with limits on distance and angle, so the camera stays above the ground and can't lose the
  keep.
- A room button flies the camera to that room's view in about a second (with reduced motion it cuts). Follow keeps the
  agent in view as it walks, until the camera is moved by hand or Follow is pressed again.
- A click on an agent is a raycast on its figure. The HUD's agent buttons do the same thing for the keyboard and screen
  readers, so nothing needs the mouse; the arrow keys pan when the canvas has focus.

## Tech

**Decided:**

- three.js as an npm dependency, pinned to an exact version and bundled by Vite. No CDN at runtime: the page still loads
  only from its own hall (`connect-src 'self'`), and the hall's one network call stays the GitHub one.
- The scene is its own module; the HUD stays React.
- Without WebGL, the page falls back to today's 2D hall.
- Rendering pauses while the tab is hidden; the pixel ratio is capped at 2.

**Proposed:**

- **Where the code goes:** `src/world/` holds pure TypeScript: the keep's layout, the path graph, the state-to-place
  map, idle life's schedule and the camera's views, all tested in Vitest with no WebGL. `src/world/scene/` holds three.js
  alone. React talks to the scene through one small interface (mount, update with the office state, select, follow, fly
  to a room, dispose). The scene never imports React, and React never touches a three object.
- **Loaded on demand:** the scene and three come in through a dynamic `import()`, so the HUD and the 2D fallback don't
  wait for them. three will be the biggest part of the page; 4a measures it and writes it down.
- **WebGL 2:** three.js renders with WebGL 2 only. The page asks for a WebGL 2 context first. With none, with a scene that
  fails to load, or with a lost context that doesn't come back, it shows the 2D hall. jsdom has no canvas context
  (`setup.ts` returns null), so every test that exists today keeps running on the 2D hall.
- **The 2D hall is frozen** as the fallback: it keeps working and keeps its tests, but new world features aren't drawn
  there. That works because of the accessibility rule: everything the world shows is also in the HUD.
- **Cel shading and outlines:** `MeshToonMaterial` with a small gradient made in code, and ink outlines from three's
  `OutlineEffect` (inverted hulls). The primitives get them in 4b, so the style is judged before any model exists.
- **Performance:** one loop through `renderer.setAnimationLoop`, stopped on `visibilitychange` and restarted on return;
  `Math.min(devicePixelRatio, 2)`; few real lights (fires and candles as glowing materials, one or two lights doing the
  work); soft blob shadows under the agents instead of shadow maps. 4a measures a frame on this PC at 100%, 125% and
  150% Windows scaling and writes the numbers down.
- **Labels:** name tags and bubbles stay HTML over the canvas, placed from 3D each frame (three's `CSS2DRenderer`, or the
  same in our own code), `aria-hidden`, with today's overlap rules (`settleLabels()`) and rem minimums. They are moved
  through `element.style`, which the CSP allows, never through a `style` attribute.

## Models

**Decided:** glTF / GLB files in the repo, made from Dima's references, each listed in `docs/ASSETS.md` with its author
and licence. Until they arrive, primitive figures.

**Proposed:**

- **Primitive figures (4b):** each agent built from shapes in code, chibi (a big head, a small body), keeping the cast's
  size order (the goblin smallest, the orcs biggest) and what each carries today (the two swords, the forge hammer, the
  Reviewer's spectacles and candle), in its own colours, cel-shaded and outlined.
- **What a model needs**, written down for whoever makes them: one GLB per character, rigged, with these animations: idle,
  walk, sit and work (typing; for the orcs, the keyboard smash), read, raise hand, and its idle life (spar, drink, hammer;
  paint; read; the Lead's wander is its walk). Colours come from materials or vertex colours (see Security). A size
  budget: under 2 MB per character and under 10 MB for all models, because the plugin install and the hosted demo carry
  every byte, and a binary in git stays in its history forever.
- **References are references.** The models are our own characters in the cast's roles. A reference image of someone
  else's character guides the style, never a copy: original fantasy only, no game's or show's characters, names, emblems
  or art. The style rules stay as they are.
- **No Git LFS.** A plugin installed from GitHub is a git clone, and whether a clone fetches LFS files depends on the PC's
  git setup. Plain files avoid that question, so models are committed when final and changed rarely.
- `.gitattributes` gets `*.glb binary`, so no line-ending conversion ever touches one. `PAGE_TYPES` gets `.glb` as
  `model/gltf-binary`, and `readManifest()` keeps refusing everything else. 4f checks that `tests/invisible.test.ts`
  leaves binary files out.
- **Question** (before 4e, not before 4a): who makes the models and their animations from the references? Claude Code
  builds the scene, the primitives, the loader and the checks, but it can't sculpt or rig detailed anime characters.
  Every option puts the same duty in `docs/ASSETS.md`:

| Option                                 | Good                            | Watch                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Dima in Blender**                    | His own, and fits the portfolio | Time: five rigged characters with about eight animations each                                          |
| **An artist**                          | Detailed and consistent         | Cost; a written licence that allows a public MIT repo and later changes                                |
| **A 3D generator from his references** | Fast                            | Its terms for the output (some plans keep rights or need credit); the rig and the mesh often need work |
| **A rigging and animation library**    | Animations at once              | Its terms must allow shipping the files in a public repo; chibi proportions may not auto-rig           |

## Security

**Decided:** models are our own bundled files only, loaded with three's `GLTFLoader`. No remote models, no eval, and the
CSP stays as it is.

**Proposed**, to keep "the CSP stays as it is" true:

- **No images packed into a GLB.** `GLTFLoader` turns an image packed inside a GLB into a `blob:` URL and loads it from
  there, and the policy (`default-src`, `img-src` and `connect-src`, all `'self'`) doesn't allow `blob:`. Material
  colours suit cel shading anyway. If a texture is ever needed, it is its own PNG beside the model, served from the
  page's own origin. 4a checks this in Edge with two test models under the policy: one with a packed image must fail,
  and one without must load. If that turns out wrong, `blob:` still doesn't go into the policy without Dima's say.
- **No compressed models:** no Draco, Meshopt or KTX2. Their decoders are WebAssembly, and Draco's runs in a worker made
  from a blob, so they would need `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` or `worker-src blob:`.
- three.js uses no `eval` and no `new Function`. 4a checks it the way Zod's jitless mode was checked: the hosted demo's
  meta policy reports nothing.
- **Nothing an agent sends becomes a model, a texture, a URL or a 3D text mesh.** Agent text stays React text in the HUD
  and in HTML bubbles. A model's URL comes from our bundle (Vite's `?url` import), never from an event.
- **The dependency:** an exact pin (no `^`), `npm audit` clean, and any install script blocked like every package's (three
  should have none). `@types/three` is pinned to match. An upgrade is a task of its own.
- The Pages demo carries the same policy as a meta tag, and the scene must load there too.

## Accessibility

**Decided:** the canvas gets a text alternative, everything it shows is also in the HUD and the feed, and name tags are
`aria-hidden`.

**Proposed:**

- The canvas has `role="img"` and a short, fixed label ("The Forge Keep, where the agents work. Their states are in the
  agent list.") that points to the HUD's agent list. It never gets a label that changes each frame. The `aria-live` region
  keeps saying what it says today.
- Every state the world shows is in the agent list and card: where the agent is, what it does, whether it needs you or
  failed. Idle life is flavour and isn't announced.
- Keyboard: room buttons, agent buttons, Follow, the drawer and the banner, in a sensible tab order. Esc closes the card
  or the drawer and puts the focus back, as the Review board does.
- Reduced motion, as decided: camera cuts, agents jump to their places, no idle animation. The furnace's colour still
  changes.
- Narrator over the HUD in the self-check, as in v3.6.

  licence, the `.glb` type in the bundled hall's manifest, the README's pictures again, the self-check in Edge at three
  Windows scalings, and Dima's list.

**Proposed tests** across the parts (Vitest, no WebGL):

- **Place map:** every state of every agent has a place; done and idle never land at a work spot; a work event moves an
  idle agent at once.
- **Path graph:** every area can be reached, and every path's ends sit in their areas.
- **Idle life:** never in a work room or at the Forge.
- **Camera:** every room view sits inside the camera's limits.
- **Fallback:** the 2D hall is chosen when there is no WebGL 2 context.
- **HUD:** React tests.
- **Bundled hall:** `.glb` is in `PAGE_TYPES` and in its manifest.

The 3D itself is checked in Edge through playwright-core, as in v3.9 and v3.10: a screenshot per room, no console
errors, and nothing reported by the page's policy. Headless Edge draws WebGL in software, so it checks that things work,
not how fast; the frame times come from a real window.
